Executive Summary: The 3 Structural Crises

ACTA by Resence is an institutional-grade, transparency-first micro-donation and civic engagement network connecting university students with manually vetted local non-profits across India. It directly resolves three structural dysfunctions:

1. The Ground Proof Deficit (67.5% Trust Collapse)

Donors receive generic email thank-yous or 80G tax slips, but zero visual evidence that food reached a hungry stomach or a tree was planted. Independent research indicates that 67.5% of educated urban youth refuse to donate to online charities due to corruption fears. ACTA mandates geotagged video proof within 96 hours before escrow release.

2. The "Chillar" Stigma & Minimum Ticket Barrier

Traditional charities (Ketto, Milaap) ignore donations below ₹500, treating small sums as "chillar". A college student on a ₹3,000 monthly allowance feels socially embarrassed to give ₹20. ACTA normalizes and celebrates ₹10 micro-giving via UPI, where 1,000 students pooling ₹10 generate ₹10,000 to feed 285 people.

3. The Retention & Community Void

Charity in India is treated as an episodic, guilt-driven transaction. ACTA introduces an engaging collegiate habit loop—institutional campus communities, streaks, action-based experience points (XP), and dignified impact tiers.

01. Product Overview, Heritage & Tagline

1.1 Project Specification

ACTA is an autonomous civic-tech network developed under parent entity Resence (https://resence.in). It is designed to bridge the trust chasm between India's 400M+ youth and genuine grassroots non-profits through cryptographic verification, video telemetry, and escrow custody.

1.2 Classical Latin Root & Historical Heritage

In Classical Latin, Acta is the plural form of Actum, translating to "Things Done", "Deeds Accomplished", or "Official Public Records."

Around 131 BC, the Roman Republic established the Acta Diurna ("Daily Deeds"). Carved in stone or metal and hung in the public Forum, it was civilization's very first daily public bulletin, ensuring citizens could verify daily state announcements, grain allotments, and civic decrees with their own eyes.

1.3 Fusion with Locked Tagline: "Small Acts. Big Impact."

The locked tagline is "Small Acts. Big Impact." The word "Acts" in the tagline is the exact English translation of the Latin root Acta. Every ₹10 micro-donation is not an anonymous rupee transfer; it is an individual Acta—a recorded, verified deed of human compassion.

1.4 The Corporate Ecosystem Architecture

ACTA mirrors an Alphabet-Google structure: Resence is the parent technology lab providing AI, design, and escrow governance; ACTA (https://acta.resence.in) is the consumer-facing civic platform.

03. Gen Z Giving & The Embarrassment Barrier

3.1 The Psychology of the College Donor

Gen Z represents India's most socially vocal demographic. They care intensely about animal welfare, hunger eradication, and environmental greening. Yet, traditional charity fails to engage them due to a critical barrier: The Social Embarrassment of Small Money.

The "Chillar" Stigma

A college student surviving on a monthly hostel allowance of ₹3,000 cannot donate ₹500 to a hospital campaign. Walking up to an offline donation desk to give ₹20 feels embarrassing because traditional non-profits dismiss small sums as petty change.

3.2 The Dignity of Micro-Giving

ACTA completely reframes small contributions. On ACTA, a ₹10 donation is celebrated with full dignity and 100 XP! We educate youth on democratic micro-giving: "Your ₹10 alone may be small, but when 1,000 GL Bajaj students pool ₹10, it creates ₹10,000—delivering 285 hot meals or planting 50 trees."

3.3 UPI 0% MDR: The Technical Enabler

This micro-giving economy is powered by a massive regulatory breakthrough: The Government of India mandates 0% Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on UPI P2M transactions under Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007. A ₹10 donation incurs ₹0.00 gateway cost, allowing 100% of the student's money to reach the escrow vault intact.

07. 3-Portal Architecture & Zero-Trust Security

To ensure bulletproof operational security and prevent data contamination, ACTA strictly isolates user roles into three independent operational portals:

1. Public User Portal (`acta.resence.in`)

Publicly accessible to any user worldwide via modern web browsers and mobile devices. Zero barriers to entry. Users browse the 7 active causes, execute 1-click UPI donations, review personal impact counters, and view the public YouTube proof archive.

2. NGO Partner Portal (Invite-Only via Magic Link)

Completely invisible to the general public. No open registration button. Once an NGO is vetted physically offline, a cryptographically signed magic link is dispatched to their official email. Houses the Director Financial Dashboard and the Volunteer Field Team Dashboard.

3. Secret Admin Control Panel (Internal Security Engine)

Operates on an unindexed, obscure route protected by strong credentials, IP whitelisting, and two-factor authentication. Core team manages causes, inspects AI frame audits, verifies merchant receipts, and triggers escrow disbursements or automated refunds.

13. Payment Logic: ₹10+ UPI & 0% MDR Math

ACTA integrates directly with Razorpay Smart UPI Gateway. When a student taps "Donate ₹10", the app triggers a native UPI Intent flow, launching Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, or BHIM directly without demanding card numbers or bank passwords.

13.2 The 0% MDR Regulatory Moat

Under Section 10A of the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (enacted by the Ministry of Finance and NPCI), **no bank or payment aggregator may charge Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) on UPI transactions**. This regulatory foundation is the economic cornerstone of ACTA:

Micro-Payment Economic Comparison

Credit Card Donation: ₹10 donation − 2% gateway fee − ₹2 minimum charge = ₹7.80 net (22% loss!).
ACTA UPI Donation: ₹10 donation − 0% MDR = ₹10.00 Net In Escrow (100% Intact!)

14. Webhook Idempotency & Network Drop Handling

14.1 Webhook Idempotency & Deduplication

Process Event (payment.captured) await handlePaymentCaptured(req.body.payload.payment.entity); return res.status(200).json({ status: 'success' }); });
